Essential Economics diagrams

Production possibility frontier (PPF)

Shows maximum possible combinations of two goods. Points on the curve are productively efficient; points inside indicate unemployed or inefficiently used resources; points outside are currently unattainable. A pivot or outward shift can show growth in productive potential.

Demand and supply

A rightward demand shift raises equilibrium price and quantity, ceteris paribus. A rightward supply shift lowers equilibrium price and raises quantity.

Negative production externality

Where production creates external costs, marginal social cost exceeds marginal private cost. The free market may overproduce relative to the socially efficient quantity, creating a welfare loss.

Aggregate demand and aggregate supply

An increase in AD shifts the curve right, raising real output and the price level in the short run, with the size of each effect depending on spare capacity and the shape of SRAS.

Long-run aggregate supply (classical)

Classical LRAS is vertical at the economy's productive potential. Policies that improve productivity, skills, technology or capital can shift LRAS right.
